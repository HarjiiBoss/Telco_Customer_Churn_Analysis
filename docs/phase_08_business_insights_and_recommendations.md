# Phase 8 — Business Insights & Recommendations

**Objective**

Translate SQL and dashboard findings into actionable business recommendations. This phase bridges technical analysis and business decision-making by identifying the key drivers of customer churn, quantifying their financial impact, and proposing evidence-based actions to improve retention and reduce revenue risk.

---

# Executive Summary

The analysis identified three primary business challenges:

- High customer churn concentrated among month-to-month contracts and early-tenure customers.
- Significant revenue exposure driven by the loss of higher-value customers.
- Structural concentration risk, with a large proportion of revenue dependent on high-churn customer segments.

Together, these factors place approximately **£1.67 million in annualised revenue at risk**.

---

# Business Insights

## Customer Insights

### Insight 1 — Month-to-month contracts drive churn

**Finding**

Month-to-month customers account for **42.71% churn**, almost four times higher than one-year contracts and over fifteen times higher than two-year contracts.

**Business Impact**

The largest portion of the customer base is also the least stable, increasing acquisition costs and reducing customer lifetime value.

---

### Insight 2 — Churn is concentrated during the first year

**Finding**

Customers within their first 12 months churn at **47.44%**, the highest rate across all tenure groups.

**Business Impact**

Customer onboarding and early engagement represent the highest-leverage opportunity for improving retention.

---

### Insight 3 — Payment behaviour signals churn risk

**Finding**

Customers using electronic checks experience a **45.29% churn rate**, substantially higher than customers using automatic payment methods.

**Business Impact**

Payment preference can be used as an early behavioural indicator for proactive retention campaigns.

---

# Revenue Insights

## Insight 1 — Churn disproportionately affects high-value customers

**Finding**

Customers who churn pay **£13.17 more per month** on average than retained customers.

**Business Impact**

Revenue loss is driven by valuable customers rather than low-spending customers, increasing the financial impact of churn.

---

## Insight 2 — Long-term customers generate substantially higher value

**Finding**

Two-year contracts generate approximately **2.7× more revenue per customer** than month-to-month contracts.

**Business Impact**

Increasing contract duration improves customer lifetime value while reducing churn exposure.

---

### Insight 3 — Losing long-term customers is especially costly

**Finding**

Churned two-year customers have an estimated **LTV of £5,316**, more than five times that of churned month-to-month customers.

**Business Impact**

Retaining established customers delivers a significantly greater financial return than replacing them.

---

# Risk Insights

## Insight 1 — Revenue is concentrated in the least stable contract type

**Finding**

Month-to-month contracts represent **55% of customers** and **56% of total monthly revenue**.

**Business Impact**

The business has a structural dependency on its highest-risk customer segment.

---

## Insight 2 — Fiber optic combines high revenue with high churn

**Finding**

Fiber optic contributes **62% of monthly revenue** while recording a **42% churn rate**.

**Business Impact**

The company's largest revenue stream is simultaneously its most volatile.

---

## Insight 3 — High-risk customers are identifiable before churn

**Finding**

A segment of **814 customers** exhibits all three risk indicators (month-to-month contract, tenure below 12 months, and monthly charges above £70) and churns at **69.53%**, compared with **20.92%** for the standard customer base.

**Business Impact**

Targeted retention campaigns can focus on a clearly defined customer group instead of broad, untargeted interventions.

---

# Recommended Actions

| Priority | Recommendation | Business Rationale |
|-----------|----------------|--------------------|
| High | Encourage migration from month-to-month to longer-term contracts through pricing incentives and loyalty offers. | Reduces exposure to the highest-churn contract segment while increasing customer lifetime value. |
| High | Strengthen onboarding and engagement during the first 12 months. | Addresses the period where churn is highest and interventions are likely to have the greatest impact. |
| High | Launch proactive retention campaigns targeting the 814 high-risk customers. | Focuses resources on customers with a 69.53% observed churn rate. |
| Medium | Review the Fiber optic customer experience, pricing, and service quality. | Protects the company's largest revenue-generating service from continued customer losses. |
| Medium | Promote automatic payment methods through incentives or discounts. | Encourages customer behaviours associated with lower churn rates. |
| Medium | Monitor contract and revenue concentration metrics regularly. | Reduces long-term structural exposure and improves business resilience. |

---

# Conclusion

The analysis demonstrates that churn is not randomly distributed across the customer base. Instead, it is concentrated among identifiable customer segments characterised by short contract terms, early tenure, premium services, and specific payment behaviours.

By prioritising retention initiatives for these high-risk segments, the business can reduce revenue exposure, improve customer lifetime value, and strengthen long-term revenue stability.
